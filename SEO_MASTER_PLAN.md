## 1. EXECUTIVE VERDICT

**Honest assessment:** EPOCH is a technically clean, 30-page site competing against 24-year-old domains (1st Commercial Credit), 55-year-old brands (Riviera), and 390-URL content machines (Porter Capital). We will **not** win "invoice factoring" or "asset-based lending" head terms in 12 months — NerdWallet, BofA, and JPMorgan own those, and we should not spend a dollar chasing them.

**But the #1 position is genuinely reachable in our actual market**, for three reasons:

1. **Every competitor is positioned down-market.** All four teardowns show SMB factoring shops ($50K–$20M revenue clients, "Apply Now!" CTA spam, 0.69% rate banners). Nobody owns the middle-market/CFO/PE-sponsor keyword layer: "middle market asset-based lending" (where EPOCH's blog *already ranks*), "non-bank ABL lender," "bridge financing for acquisitions," "senior secured credit facility," "unitranche," "strategic capital." These SERPs are held by PDFs, glossary stubs, and regional bank pages. We can own this entire tier in **6–9 months**.
2. **Every competitor has thin schema and zero AI-search discipline.** No FAQPage, LocalBusiness, or Service markup at 1stCC, SouthStar, or Riviera. AI Overviews cite structured, number-rich definitional content — a format none of them produce and EPOCH's stack (Next.js + seo.yaml contract) is built to ship.
3. **The geo game is a template war we can win on quality.** Winners run 60–80% boilerplate location pages. Google's helpful-content systems punish exactly that. Fewer, deeper, genuinely localized pages beat their footprint.

**We also have a self-inflicted wound bleeding equity right now:** ~130 legacy WordPress 301s in next.config.js point to /blog/ slugs that don't exist — 301→404 chains torching a decade of link equity. Fixing this is worth more than any single new page.

## 2. THE 5 PATTERNS COMPETITORS USE TO WIN

### Pattern 1 — Geo hub-and-spoke at scale (1stCC: 50 states + 90 cities; Porter: 50 states)
State pages at ~2,500–4,000 words targeting "[service] in [state]," city pages targeting "[service] [city]," all cross-linked to services and industries. This is the single biggest traffic engine in the category.
**EPOCH move: replicate, but better.** They run 60–80% boilerplate and target only "invoice factoring [state]" — leaving **"asset-based lending [state]" and "AR financing [city]" open**. We build 40%+ unique pages targeting both keyword sets, in waves, with FAQ + areaServed schema they all lack.

### Pattern 2 — Industry vertical pages with exact-match slugs (all four competitors)
~10 verticals each, 1,000–4,250 words, industry pain points + advance rates + case proof, cross-linked from every service and location page. Porter's oil & gas page is 4,250 words with 13 H2s.
**EPOCH move: replicate at the middle-market altitude.** We have 14 industry pages already — but no industry-specific *service* pages (AR for manufacturers, payroll funding for staffing, govcon receivables). SERP research shows these are held by low-DR niche lenders. Out-depth them.

### Pattern 3 — Deal tombstones as ranking assets (SouthStar's formula)
"$X [Product] Facility for [City]-Based [Industry] Company" posts do triple duty: long-tail geo+industry keywords, social proof, and trade-press backlink bait (ABL Advisor-type pickups).
**EPOCH move: replicate exactly.** Every closed engagement becomes an anonymized tombstone article published on the relevant city page AND as a standalone post linking to it. This is SouthStar's proven Charlotte tactic and it's cheap.

### Pattern 4 — Dense hierarchical internal linking (1stCC's discipline, Porter's 100+ links/page)
1stCC's AR page links to 11 industries, 9 services, 7 locations, 5 posts. Porter's state pages link to every service and industry page. This siloing is how mid-DR sites build topical authority head terms can't ignore.
**EPOCH move: replicate.** Our body-level contextual linking is thin (32 links to /contact, near-orphan geo and CRE pages). Every page must link laterally within its silo and vertically to hub/parent.

### Pattern 5 — Extractable numbers as snippet/AI bait (1stCC's "0.69%–1.59%", winners' advance-rate tables)
Rate ranges, advance rates (75–90%), funding timelines (24–48h), deal sizes ($5M–$100M) stated early and repeatedly win featured snippets and AI citations. Zero competitors pair this with pricing *transparency content* ("how much does X cost") — a gap.
**EPOCH move: beat.** EPOCH already publishes 85–90% advance rates and 0.75–1.50% fees — rare citable facts. Lead every page with them, wrap in question-form H2s + FAQ schema, and add the cost/calculator content nobody in the category has.

**The one pattern we do NOT copy:** CTA spam and downmarket design. EPOCH's institutional positioning is the conversion differentiator for CFO/sponsor traffic — credibility converts this audience, not 15 "Apply Now" buttons.

## 6. AI-SEARCH PLAYBOOK (AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity)

The research is unambiguous: finance AI answers cite **structured, number-rich, definitional pages** — not homepages, not service brochures. Reddit doesn't dominate this vertical, so authoritative pages actually win. EPOCH is currently cited **zero** times across all three clusters. The fix:

1. **Lead every page with an extractable answer.** 40–60 word definition or direct answer in the first paragraph, before any brand copy. This is the unit AI engines lift.
2. **State numbers early and precisely.** EPOCH's 85–90% advance rates and 0.75–1.50% fee range are rare, citable facts — put them in the first screen of every relevant page, in a table. Add deal sizes ($1M–$50M+), funding timelines (24–48h), typical terms. Pages with concrete parameters are what got Saratoga, Golub, and KORE Capital cited.
3. **Question-form H2s everywhere** ("What is a borrowing base?", "How fast can a Texas company get an AR facility?") + FAQPage schema (technical fix #2). Bulleted/tabular structure — structured pages are ~30% likelier to be cited.
4. **Cover both retrieval paths.** Perplexity and AI Overviews pull from organic rankings → our classic SEO work feeds them directly. ChatGPT leans on consensus definitional sources → our glossary hub (backlog #58) and definition-first explainers feed that. Only ~11% of domains get cited by both engines; we need both content shapes.
5. **Own the queries where we're the only expert source.** AI answers cite niche pages when nobody else has the mechanics: Assignment of Claims Act (piece backlog #53), borrowing-base ineligibles (#44), payroll funding cycles (#23), ABL covenant mechanics (#50). These are our fastest citations — target them before definitional head terms.
6. **Get into the lists AI recommends from.** For "best middle-market ABL lenders / non-bank lenders" queries, AI engines cite directory listicles (Dakota, GrowthCap-style, altfundsglobal). Action items: Axial profile, SFNet membership + directory listing, ABF Journal / ABL Advisor deal announcements (tombstone PR doubles here), pitch inclusion in existing "top middle-market lender" roundups. This is the only off-page work that directly changes AI answers.
7. **Freshness + authorship signals:** visible "Updated [Month Year]" on every cluster page, named advisor bylines with credentials, Person schema. AI engines demonstrably prefer dated, attributed finance content.
8. **Monthly AI-citation audit:** run the 15 scoreboard queries below through Google AI Overview, ChatGPT (search mode), and Perplexity; log which domains are cited; track EPOCH's first citation as a KPI alongside rankings.
